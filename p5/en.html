Vertikis in p5.js

This is the short version of the vertikis: a single file, sketch.js, with about 300 lines of p5.js. No sound, no frights: only what makes them behave like soft balloons. And that fits in five ideas.

A vertiki is a ring of points

There's no hidden «soft body»: each vertiki is 24 points placed in a circle. What you see is a smooth curve that runs through all of them (splineVertex), with a face painted on top, at its centre. Everything else is deciding, 60 times a second, where each point goes.

Speed is where you came from

The points don't store their speed. They store where they were on the previous step, and the speed is the difference. It's called Verlet integration, and it has one huge advantage: if something pushes a point, a collision or an edge, the speed fixes itself on the next step. 

const vx = (p.x - p.oldX) * f;
const vy = (p.y - p.oldY) * f;
p.oldX = p.x;
p.oldY = p.y;
p.x += vx;
p.y += vy + GRAVITY;

f is friction: how much speed is kept on each step (96 %). Without it, the vertikis would bounce forever.

Remembering the shape

With only inertia and gravity, the points would each fall their own way. The trick that holds them together is called shape matching: on every step we work out where the vertiki is (the average of its points) and how much it has turned, place its resting circle there, and each point pulls a little towards its spot on that circle. 

fitShape(v);
v.points.forEach((p, i) => {
  const a = (i / POINTS) * TWO_PI + v.angle;
  p.x += (v.cx + cos(a) * v.r - p.x) * STIFFNESS;
  p.y += (v.cy + sin(a) * v.r - p.y) * STIFFNESS;
});

STIFFNESS is the stiffness slider above. At 0.05 the vertikis are jelly and ooze; at 0.9 they're rubber balls. The turn matters: without it, a rolling vertiki would snap back to its shape, as if an invisible hand set it upright.

Colliding with two simple rules

Working out exact collisions between two soft curves is hard. It isn't done here. Instead there are two rules that, together, look like a real collision:

One: every point that gets inside another vertiki, seen as a circle, is pushed back out. Only the touching points move, and that's why they squash right where they meet. 

const d = dist(p.x, p.y, b.cx, b.cy);
if (d >= R || d === 0) continue;
const push = (R - d) * 0.5;
p.x += ((p.x - b.cx) / d) * push;
p.y += ((p.y - b.cy) / d) * push;

Two: if two centres are closer than their sizes allow, both vertikis move apart as a whole, each according to its weight (its area). The one you're holding weighs six times more, so you can push your way through the others.

Many small steps

Every frame the physics runs four times in a row (SUBSTEPS), in small steps. One big step lets points sink too far into each other before they're corrected, and everything jitters. Four small ones, each with its own correction, look firm and smooth.

The rest is character

The face tilts with the body, but only by a third, so it's never upside down. The eyes follow the pointer and blink every few seconds, and the smile widens when you hover over it. A tap inflates the vertiki by 15 %: if it gets too big, it bursts into falling drops. Leave it alone and it slowly deflates. And a double-click on an empty spot gives birth to a small one that grows to its size.

Change it yourself

Here’s the whole sketch.js. Change anything: the result on the right runs again as soon as you stop typing (or with ⌘/Ctrl + Enter). Try setting POINTS to 6, making gravity negative or putting other colours in PALETTE.

Take it with you

Open editor.p5js.org, paste sketch.js over the example sketch and press ▶. It works as is, full screen. Try changing the numbers at the top of the file, the colours or the face. For instance: what if gravity pointed at the mouse?

It uses p5.js 2. On p5.js 1.x, swap splineVertex for curveVertex (and repeat the first three points at the end to close the curve). The comments in sketch.js are in Spanish.

The full version is written without libraries and adds synthesised sound, phone tilt, frights and pixel mode. The README has a tutorial that builds it step by step.
